Reviewer comments on "Auditing Algorithmic Hiring Tools"

Below are my comments, keyed to the page where the passage appears.

1. (p. 1) The About This section says the report is written for agency staff
   with limited technical capacity, but the Capacity section concludes that
   running the framework requires two technically trained staff per regional
   office. These two statements are in tension. Please reconcile them: either
   the framework is usable by the audience as described, or the report is
   really an argument for hiring, and it should say which.

2. (p. 2) The claim that vendors have "converged on a narrow reading" of Local
   Law 144 is stated without support. This is an empirical claim about vendor
   behavior and it carries a lot of the report's motivation. Please cite the
   published audits you are characterizing, say how many you looked at, and
   note whether you looked at all of them or a sample.

3. (p. 3) Layer 2 is the most original contribution here and also the least
   developed. Three things need to happen to it. First, define construct
   validity for a reader who has not taken a psychometrics course, because the
   intended audience has not. Second, say what evidence would satisfy the
   layer, since "require the validation evidence" is not an operational
   instruction and an auditor will need a threshold. Third, reconcile the layer
   with the Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures, which already
   impose validation requirements and which the report does not mention at all.

4. (p. 3) The prosody example is good but it is doing double duty as
   illustration and as evidence. I would keep it as an illustration and add a
   real audit finding if one is available in the public record.

5. (p. 4) The recommendation to treat a refusal to produce validation evidence
   as "itself a finding" is the most consequential proposal in the report and
   it gets two sentences. What is the legal basis? Under what authority would
   an agency publish an adverse finding based on non-production rather than on
   evidence of disparate impact? A reader at an enforcement agency will stop
   here.

6. (p. 4) Layer 3 asserts that the same tool can be lawful in one deployment
   and unlawful in another. I agree, but the report should say whether it means
   this as a description of current doctrine or as an argument about how
   doctrine should develop. As written a reader could take it for settled law.

7. (p. 5) Recommendation 4 asks legislatures to fund staff before expanding
   mandates. Nothing in the report estimates what that funding would cost
   beyond "two staff per regional office", and the report does not say how many
   regional offices are in scope. A rough total would make this recommendation
   actionable instead of rhetorical.

8. (p. 5) There is no reference list.

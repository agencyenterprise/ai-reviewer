Review of "Five Concepts for Municipal Heat Resilience"

Thank you for the chance to read this. It is well organized and I expect city
staff will find it usable. My comments follow the order of the report.

On the framing, around page 2, the report says extreme heat kills more people
than any other weather hazard "in most temperate cities" but does not say over
what period or from whose mortality accounting. Heat death counts vary by a
factor of five depending on whether they use coded causes or excess mortality.
Please say which you mean.

Concept 2 is the most useful idea in the report and I would like it to be
first. The overnight minimum point reorganizes how a reader thinks about the
whole problem, and readers who stop after two concepts should have it.

Concept 4 is where I have my most substantive concern. The claim that cities
which capitalize shade funding have "canopy survival rates roughly double" is
the strongest empirical claim in the report and it appears without a source,
a sample, or a definition of survival. If this rests on a handful of cities,
say so. As written it will be quoted back at city councils as an established
finding.

Still on the fourth concept, the argument treats the operating-versus-capital
distinction as though it works the same way everywhere. In many states,
capital budgets are constrained by debt limits and by rules about what
qualifies as a capital asset, and street trees often do not qualify. The
recommendation may be unavailable to a large share of the report's audience.
A paragraph on when this move is legally possible would make the concept
usable rather than aspirational.

Concept 3 says cooling centers are "the least effective per dollar" municipal
response. Compared with what, measured how? This is the kind of claim that
needs either a citation or a hedge, and it sits awkwardly next to
Recommendation 5, which tells cities to maintain them at current levels. If
they are the least effective use of money, why is the recommendation to keep
funding them?

Around page 8, in the section that puts the concepts together, the report says
the sequence "takes a decade". That number appears nowhere else and is not
derived. Either support it or drop it.

Finally, the report never discusses cost. Five concepts, five
recommendations, and no indication of what any of it costs or where the money
comes from. For an audience of city managers this is the first question they
will ask.

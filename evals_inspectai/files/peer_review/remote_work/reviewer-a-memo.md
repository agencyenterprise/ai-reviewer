# Review of "Distributed Work and Team Productivity: Evidence from a Two-Year Panel"

Reviewer: A
Recommendation: Revise and resubmit

## Overall assessment

This is a careful piece of work on a question where careful work has been
scarce. The panel design is the right choice and the authors are honest about
the size of what they found. My concerns are about framing and about how much
of the report is spent on machinery that the intended audience will not read.

## Framing

- The title promises evidence about productivity, but the report measures a
  constructed index that is mostly about throughput and latency. I would either
  rename the index something narrower or say plainly in the About This section
  that "productivity" here means this specific index and nothing else.

- The report is addressed to a general policy audience, but the Background
  section assumes the reader already knows why cross-sectional designs are
  suspect. Two sentences explaining selection bias in plain terms would carry
  most readers the rest of the way.

## Methods

- The Methods section is far longer than this audience needs. The
  standardization procedure, the clustering choice, and the three robustness
  checks are all defensible, but they belong in an appendix. In the body I
  would keep the panel structure, the outcome measure in one paragraph, and the
  fixed effects design in two sentences. As it stands the reader has to get
  through a page and a half of estimation detail before reaching a single
  number.

- Voluntary recruitment through an industry consortium is a real limitation and
  it is mentioned only in passing. Firms that join consortia and agree to be
  measured are not a random draw from the population of firms.

## Interpretation

- The claim that the cost is "a coordination cost rather than an effort cost"
  is doing a lot of work and rests entirely on which component of the index
  moved. Cycle time can rise for reasons that have nothing to do with
  coordination, including reviewers being asked to cover more surface area. I
  would soften this to a hypothesis.

- The team-age finding is presented as the most suggestive result and then
  turned directly into Recommendation 2, despite the authors saying the
  difference is not statistically significant. Either the finding supports the
  recommendation or it does not. I would drop the recommendation or reframe it
  explicitly as a hypothesis worth testing.

- Recommendation 3 tells firms to invest in reducing review and deployment
  latency. Nothing in the study tests whether such investment would recover the
  lost productivity, so this is an inference beyond the data.

## Minor comments

- The confidence interval on the headline estimate touches -0.01, and the text
  says it "touches zero". It does not quite; please state the interval and let
  the reader judge.

- "Available on request" is not an acceptable reference list for a report that
  makes empirical claims about prior literature.

Review of the distributed work panel report

I read this with interest and I think the underlying analysis is sound. My
comments are mostly about what the report leaves out rather than what it gets
wrong.

The Methods section needs more detail, not less. The sampling frame is
described in a single sentence: 84 teams at 12 firms recruited through an
industry consortium. I cannot tell from this how the 84 teams were selected
within those 12 firms, whether every team at a participating firm was included,
or what happened to teams that dissolved or merged during the eight quarters.
Given that identification comes entirely from the 61 arrangement changes, I
also want to know what drove those changes. If teams moved to distributed work
because they were already struggling, the fixed effects design does not save
you. A short subsection on how arrangement changes came about would do more for
my confidence in the result than any of the robustness checks currently
reported.

Relatedly, the three sectors studied are all ones where software is a cost
center or a regulated function. I would be cautious about generalizing to
firms where the software team is the product, and the report should say so.
The Recommendations are written as though they apply to firms in general.

On the outcome measure, I would like to see the three components reported
separately in the main results table rather than only in the decomposition
paragraph. Readers who distrust composite indices, and there are many of us,
should be able to read the paper without them.

I am not persuaded by the treatment of the team-age result. Splitting the
sample and finding a larger effect in one half is exactly the kind of analysis
that produces findings which do not replicate, and the authors acknowledge the
difference is not significant while continuing to build on it. At minimum this
needs a sentence about how many such splits were examined.

Two smaller things. The defect-escape rate is defined per 1,000 deployments,
which means a team that deploys less often can improve its rate by shipping
less. Some acknowledgement of that would be welcome. And the report never says
what happened to the teams that were observed for fewer than eight quarters,
though a robustness check restricts to those that were.

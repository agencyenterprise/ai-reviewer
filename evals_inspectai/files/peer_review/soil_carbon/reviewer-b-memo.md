Review

I agree with the thesis and I think it is worth publishing. Two things hold it
back.

The additionality argument is the strongest of the three and it is given the
least space. It is also the only one of the three that cannot be fixed by
better technology, which is the point that would change a reader's mind. I
would expand it and move it first.

My second concern is that the piece asserts the models used in soil carbon
protocols are "calibrated on a thin and geographically skewed set of long-term
trials" and leaves it there. This is the crux of the measurement argument. Even
in a commentary, a reader deserves to know which protocols the author has in
mind and what the calibration base actually looks like.

I will also note, without insisting on it, that the piece would read as more
even-handed if it engaged with the strongest argument on the other side, which
is that imperfect measurement plus conservative discounting can still produce
a credit that is net beneficial. The author does not have to agree, but
ignoring the discounting response leaves the argument looking narrower than it
is.

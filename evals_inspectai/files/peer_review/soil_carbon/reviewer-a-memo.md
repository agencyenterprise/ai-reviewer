REVIEWER REPORT

Manuscript ID: COMM-2024-0417
Title: Soil Carbon Credits Are Not Ready for Compliance Markets
Type: Commentary
Reviewer: A
Recommendation: Minor revision

Summary of the piece

The author argues that soil carbon credits should be kept out of compliance
markets on measurement, permanence, and additionality grounds, while continuing
to pay farmers for soil carbon on agronomic grounds. The argument is clear and
the distinction between the two payment rationales is the piece's main
contribution.

Comments

1. The measurement argument would be stronger with a number. The author says
   the annual change is "small relative to that background variation" without
   saying how small. One sentence giving a typical annual change and a typical
   within-field standard deviation would let the reader assess the claim
   directly.

2. On permanence, the claim that "a single pass of deep tillage can undo a
   decade of accumulation" is stated more strongly than the literature
   supports. Deep tillage causes large losses, but a full decade is at the
   extreme end of reported estimates. Please soften or cite.

3. The additionality section attributes rising no-till adoption to fuel costs
   and equipment. Both matter, but conservation compliance requirements have
   also driven adoption in some regions and should be mentioned.

4. The closing line is effective but the piece would benefit from one sentence
   on what a policy that follows the author's advice would actually look like.
   Readers who agree will want to know what to do on Monday.

5. Minor: "compliance markets are built on the assumption that a retired credit
   represents a permanent removal" is true of some registries and not others.
   Consider narrowing.
